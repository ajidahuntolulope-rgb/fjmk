How Should a Clinic Reply to a Negative Google Review?

A clinic should reply calmly, avoid confirming anyone’s patient status or sharing private details, and move individual concerns to a private channel.

FJMK · Healthcare design and digital practice

Short answer: Acknowledge the feedback, keep the reply brief, do not confirm that the reviewer is or was a patient, and invite them to contact the clinic through a private, appropriate channel. Never publish personal or clinical details to defend the clinic in public.

A public review creates two conversations. One is with the person who wrote it. The other is with everyone deciding whether the clinic seems attentive and trustworthy. The reply has to respect both, while protecting the privacy of the person involved.

A four-step response

  1. Acknowledge: Thank the reviewer for raising a concern without confirming a visit or treatment.
  2. Set a privacy boundary: Say that the clinic cannot discuss individual circumstances in a public reply.
  3. Offer a private route: Provide a suitable phone number or email monitored by someone who can investigate.
  4. Follow up internally: Check the issue through the clinic’s normal process and record what needs to change.

A neutral template might read: “Thank you for sharing this feedback. We take concerns seriously, but we cannot discuss individual circumstances in a public forum. Please contact [role or department] through [private contact route] so the team can understand the concern.” Adapt it to the clinic’s real process; do not promise a result or response time the team cannot meet.

What not to put in the reply

  • Do not confirm that the reviewer attended, received treatment or has a diagnosis.
  • Do not quote appointment details, records, dates or messages, even if the reviewer has included them first.
  • Do not accuse the reviewer of lying or disclose what the clinic thinks happened.
  • Do not offer a discount, gift or other incentive in exchange for changing or removing a review.
  • Do not ask only satisfied visitors for reviews or discourage negative feedback.

Google says businesses may ask customers for honest reviews based on genuine experiences, but incentives and selectively soliciting positive reviews are prohibited. Google also recommends protecting privacy and moving complex concerns into a private channel.

Is replying a shortcut to higher rankings?

No reliable source supports treating a reply as a ranking trick. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence; it notes that review count and positive ratings can contribute to prominence. Its review guidance says replies show that a business values feedback and help people understand how it responds. Those are useful reasons to reply, but they are not a guarantee of higher placement.

Make the process work before a review arrives

Choose who monitors reviews, which concerns need escalation and which private contact route is safe and staffed. Train that person not to confirm patient status in public. If a review appears to violate platform rules, use Google’s reporting process rather than arguing about it in the reply.

For a clinic, the strongest response is a calm public boundary followed by a real private follow-up. The reply cannot repair the underlying experience by itself; the clinic’s process has to do that work.
