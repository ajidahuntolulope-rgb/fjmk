Do Lagos Clinic Websites Publish a Privacy Policy and a Working Contact Route?

In a 20-provider Lagos screen, 18 displayed a contact route and seven linked to a privacy policy. Here is what clinic owners can check on their own sites.

FJMK · Healthcare design and digital practice · Published 25 October 2026

Short answer: Open your own site and check two things. First, is there a privacy notice that a patient can actually reach, explaining what happens to the information they type into a booking or contact form? Second, is there a phone, WhatsApp or booking route visible without hunting? These are separate checks. A clinic can have one and not the other, and in our sample several did.

What we checked

On 24 October 2026 we opened the home pages of 20 Lagos healthcare providers: seven general hospitals, three specialist clinics, five fertility clinics and five dental clinics. We recorded whether the captured page source contained a contact link (a phone, WhatsApp, booking or appointment link) and whether it contained a privacy-policy link.

Home-page checkResult
Pages that loaded20 / 20
Named a Lagos location or area20 / 20
Exposed a phone, WhatsApp, booking or appointment link18 / 20
Included a privacy-policy link7 / 20

The contact gap sat in the hospitals

Both pages that did not expose a contact link were general hospitals. Dental clinics, fertility clinics and specialist clinics in the sample all exposed one. With 20 providers that is a small observation rather than a citywide finding, but it points at something worth a second look: the larger and more complex the facility, the more likely the front page assumes the patient already knows how to reach the right department.

For a patient arriving from a Google search, that assumption costs the enquiry. They land, they cannot see who to call, and they go back to the results.

Most pages had no privacy link at all

Thirteen of the 20 pages had no privacy-policy link in the captured source. Five of the seven hospitals were among them.

Read that carefully. A missing link on a home page does not mean the clinic has no privacy notice, and it certainly does not mean the clinic is breaking the law. The notice may sit in a footer, on a subpage or in patient forms. The result shows that a patient looking from the front page would not have found a link in most of these cases.

A clinic site that takes a name, phone number and a description of a medical concern through a booking form is collecting personal information. Nigeria’s Data Protection Act 2023 provides the legal framework for processing personal information.

What to do about it

  1. Open your site as a patient would, on a phone, and look for a privacy link without scrolling to the footer. If you cannot find it quickly, your patients cannot either.
  2. Check what your privacy notice says about enquiry forms: what you collect, where it goes, how long you keep it and who to contact.
  3. Make sure the contact route is visible on the first screen, pointed at a number or inbox someone actually monitors.
  4. If you run more than one location, check each site or branch page separately. A group page does not cover a branch.

Research basis: FJMK’s 20-provider Lagos website screen, conducted 24 October 2026. See the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.
