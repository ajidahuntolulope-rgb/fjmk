Quick answer: In a search-derived sample of 388 healthcare listings across Lagos State, public searches surfaced no website candidate for 316 (81.4%). Three listings passed an automated identity screen; 42 candidate sites remained unconfirmed.

The 316 entries with no surfaced website point to a practical visibility gap in this sample. Here is what the results look like by area and on a phone-sized screen.

What did we find?

We searched for clinics, hospitals, pharmacies and laboratories across all 20 Lagos local government areas (LGAs), then looked for a website connected to each listing. We removed two pharmaceutical manufacturers from the patient-facing sample, leaving 388 records for the availability comparison.

Three candidate pages passed an automated screen that looked for at least two of these cues: the provider’s name, address or nearby landmark, and phone number.

The area comparison counted a website candidate when public searches surfaced one for a listing, whether or not the provider identity could be confirmed. On that measure, Ikeja had the highest share in the sample at 28%, followed by Ojo at 25% and Surulere at 21.7%. Epe had no candidate surfaced; Apapa and Lagos Island were at 3.4% and 4%.

How did the candidate pages work on a phone?

We also opened 47 candidate pages in a browser set to a 390 × 844-pixel phone viewport. Ten passed the automated screen, 28 were partial and nine failed. The screen checked the mobile viewport setting, sideways scrolling, text size, oversized images and tap-target size.

Two pages required sideways scrolling. Three used body text smaller than 14 pixels, and three lacked a mobile viewport setting. Those are specific fixes a clinic can check before commissioning a redesign.

What should a clinic take from this?

Start by searching the clinic’s name and location, then check whether the clinic controls the website that appears. A directory page or map profile can help people find basic details, but it does not replace a clear clinic-owned page for services, location, hours and the next step.

If there is a site, open it on a phone. Check that text is readable, the page fits the screen and a visitor can find the right contact route. Our guide to the pages a clinic website should have covers what to include; the Google Maps guide covers the listing side.

How we searched

We collected provider listings through area- and provider-type searches, public healthcare directories and search-surfaced map cards. After removing exact name-and-address duplicates, we grouped same-name records within the same LGA and reviewed 90 groups with address variants. We then searched for candidate websites and screened the pages we found. For each LGA, the rate is the share of its grouped search records with a candidate website.

For phone checks, the pages were loaded in a browser at 390 × 844 pixels and scored against five technical criteria.

A note on these figures

The 388 records came from public searches and directories, not a census of Lagos healthcare. “No candidate found” means no website surfaced in our searches; the automated identity screen did not confirm provider ownership, and the browser checks were not human usability tests.
