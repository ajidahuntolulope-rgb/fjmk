How to Get Your Clinic on Google Maps: The Complete Guide

OA
Oluwatobi Ajidahun
FJMK · Local search

If you run a clinic in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt and you only do one thing this week for your practice, do this: get your clinic on Google Maps. It is the single fastest, highest-leverage step you can take for local visibility. A patient who searches "clinic near me" on their phone and finds your name, your address, and a phone number is a patient you did not have to pay to reach. Nothing else you can do for local discovery costs less and pays back faster.

Why Google Maps matters for a clinic

Think about how patients actually find you. They do not read the Yellow Pages. They open their phone. About 46% of all Google searches have local intent, and in local searches the top three map-pack listings capture roughly 70% of the clicks (BrightLocal 2025 Local Consumer Survey). That map pack — the little box of three businesses above the regular results — is where patients decide who to call.

And patients start with search. Around 77% begin their hunt for care online (Google Health, directional). Someone in Surulere types "general practitioner near me," and Google shows a map, three clinics, and a phone number. If your clinic is not one of those three, the patient calls someone else.

The "near me" habit is spreading to AI. Roughly 45% of consumers now use ChatGPT or another AI tool for local recommendations — up from 6% a year earlier (BrightLocal 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey). Those tools pull their answers from your Google listing. Get the listing right and you feed both the map pack and the AI answers.

Step 1: Create or claim your profile

Go to business.google.com and create your Google Business Profile. If a listing already exists — one Google may have auto-created from other data — claim it instead of making a new one. Creating a duplicate is a common way to get suspended.

You need a real business to qualify: a real address, a real clinic, real services. Google verifies the business is genuine before it lets you manage the listing.

The Nigeria verification reality

Verification in Nigeria is not a single fixed process. Google chooses the method, and it varies. You may be asked to verify by video — recording your clinic, your signage, and your surroundings — or by a postcard sent to your address with a code. There is no picking and choosing; Google decides what it wants from you. Either way, the clinic must be real and reachable, because that is exactly what verification checks.

Step 2: Choose the right category

This is where most clinic owners lose patients without knowing it. Google matches your listing to searches by your category, not your tagline. A patient searching "dental clinic" is matched to businesses categorized as dental clinics. If you are categorized as a generic "medical clinic," you will not show up for "dental clinic."

So be specific. Pick the most specific primary category that fits: "General practitioner," "Dental clinic," "Gynaecologist," "Dermatologist." Do not settle for "Doctor" or "Medical clinic" when a more precise option exists. The more specific the category, the more targeted the searches you appear in — and targeted searches are the ones that become patients.

Step 3: Fill every field completely

An empty profile is a dead profile. Complete every field:

  • Hours. Your real opening hours, including public holidays. A patient who walks to your clinic on a holiday and finds it closed will not come back. List holiday hours.
  • Phone. The number someone actually answers. If your reception line rings and no one picks up, the patient calls the next clinic on the list.
  • Website. Link to your site. This is how a patient moves from finding you to booking.
  • Services. List what you offer. This helps Google match you to specific searches.
  • Photos. This is not cosmetic. Practices with 100+ photos get roughly 520% more calls than those with fewer than five (BrightLocal). You do not need 100 — but you need more than five. Take 10–20 real photos: your waiting room, consultation room, team, reception desk, and the exterior so people recognize the building. Patients want to see where they are going before they go.

Step 4: Keep your NAP identical everywhere

NAP is name, address, and phone. It must be spelled exactly the same on your Google listing, your website, your Facebook page, your Instagram bio, and any directory you appear in. Not "Lagos" one place and "Lagos Island" another. Not "Dr." one place and "Doctor" another.

Inconsistency confuses Google — and it confuses the AI systems that now answer local questions. When systems cannot tell whether two entries are the same clinic, they treat them as different businesses, split your reputation, and weaken both. One name, one address, one number, everywhere.

Step 5: Get reviews — and keep them fresh

Reviews are the trust signal that decides whether a patient calls you or the clinic next door. 97% of consumers use online reviews to guide decisions (BrightLocal). But here is the part most clinics miss: 73% of consumers only trust reviews from the last month (BrightLocal). Old reviews are not enough. You need a steady flow.

So ask. When a patient is happy — after a good consultation, a clean procedure, a resolved complaint — ask them to leave a review. Send the link to your review page on WhatsApp. Make it one tap. Then respond to every review, good or bad. A thank-you costs ten seconds and tells the next patient you care. A calm reply to a negative review tells them you take complaints seriously.

Never buy reviews. Google catches fake reviews, and a suspended listing removes you from the map entirely — the opposite of what you want.

Step 6: Keep it alive

A Google listing is not a set-and-forget task. It is a living page, and Google rewards businesses that keep it current.

  • Post when something changes. New doctor, new hours, new service? Update the listing. Google notices the activity.
  • Answer the Q&A section yourself. Before a stranger answers for you. Patients ask real questions there — "Do you do scans?" "What are your weekend hours?" — and if you do not answer, someone else might, with wrong information.
  • Refresh photos. Add a new photo when you renovate, hire, or change anything visible.

A listing that changes looks alive. A listing that never changes looks abandoned.

What the profile does NOT fix

Be clear about the limits. A Google profile drives discovery — it gets patients to find you and call you. It does not, by itself, convert them into booked appointments. That is the job of your website.

When a patient clicks through from your listing, they land on your website. If that site is slow, confusing, or has no clear way to book, you lose the patient you just won. A strong profile with a weak website is a leaky funnel. The two work together: the profile fills the top of the funnel, the website closes it. If you have the profile but no website, or a website that does not convert, fix that too.

Common mistakes that get clinics suspended or ignored

  • Duplicate listings. Creating a second profile when one exists. Google suspends duplicates. Claim the existing one.
  • Wrong category. Too generic to match real searches.
  • Fake address. Listing an address that is not a real, staffed clinic. Google verifies and suspends fakes.
  • Inconsistent NAP. Different names, addresses, or numbers across platforms.
  • Buying reviews. Detected and punished.
  • Ignoring the Q&A. Letting strangers answer for you, often wrongly.

How long does it take to appear on Google Maps?

Once your profile is verified, it can appear within a few days. Ranking in the top three map-pack spots takes consistent work on category, photos, reviews, and updates.

Do I need a website to get on Google Maps?

No. You can create a listing without one. But you should have a website — the profile drives discovery, and the website converts that discovery into bookings. Together they fill your schedule.

How do I verify my clinic in Nigeria?

Google chooses the method. It may be video verification — recording your clinic, signage, and surroundings — or a postcard with a code sent to your address. You cannot choose; you complete whatever Google asks.

Can I list a home-based clinic?

Only if it is a real, staffed clinic with a genuine address where you actually see patients. A residential address that is not a functioning clinic will not pass verification.

Why was my listing suspended?

The common causes: a duplicate listing, a fake or inaccurate address, bought reviews, or NAP details that contradict your website and other sources. Fix the cause, then appeal through the profile.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. What matters is recency and response. A steady flow of fresh reviews, all answered, builds more trust than a pile of old ones.

What to do next

Start with Step 1 today: claim or create your profile, and complete every field. It is free, it is yours, and it is the foundation of everything else. Then work the steps — category, NAP, photos, reviews, updates — and keep the listing alive.
