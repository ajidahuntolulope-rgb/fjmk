# FJMK — BRAND & VISUAL IDENTITY BRIEF
### For use as the master context when generating logo, poster, and brand asset prompts.

---

## 1. THE BUSINESS

**FJMK is a web agency that builds websites exclusively for clinics and hospitals.**

Not a generalist agency that "also does healthcare." Healthcare only. That single fact is the
whole positioning.

**The core promise: we fill your patient schedule.**

They do not sell websites. They sell booked appointments. The website is the machine that
produces them. Every design decision FJMK makes for a client is measured against one number:
new patients booked.

**Services:** custom web design, patient portal / online booking integration, local SEO for
healthcare, HIPAA-compliant architecture, analytics, reputation management.

**Reality check:** FJMK is a young, lean agency. The brand must look established and
expensive without pretending to be a 200-person firm. Confidence through restraint, not
through volume.

---

## 2. WHO THEY SELL TO

| Segment | Who they are | What they feel |
|---|---|---|
| Clinic owners | 1–5 doctor practices, family medicine, dental | Not short on expertise — short on patients |
| Practice managers | Own the booking problem, answer the phone | Buried, pragmatic, allergic to fluff |
| Hospital marketing leads | Bigger budgets, more process | Need to justify spend to a board |

**Emotional truth:** they have been burned by a cheap website before. They are skeptical of
agencies. They have seen a hundred vendors promise "digital transformation." They respond to
one thing: **proof that this fills slots.**

**Geographic/cultural context:** Lagos, Nigeria. West African market with international
ambition. The brand should not look like a Silicon Valley startup clone, and should not look
like a local print shop either. It should look like a firm that could take a call from Dubai
or London without embarrassment.

---

## 3. THE POSITIONING LINE (pick or remix)

- **"We fill your schedule."** — the sharpest. The whole brand can hang on this.
- "Healthcare only. Patients first."
- "The agency that speaks clinic."
- "Websites that book patients, not just look pretty."

## 4. THE GENERIC TRAP — WHAT TO BAN

This is the most important section of the brief. Healthcare + web agency is the single most
cliché-ridden combination in design. If any of the following appear, the identity has failed.

### BANNED ICONOGRAPHY
- ❌ Medical cross (any variant, any angle, any "clever" interpretation)
- ❌ Stethoscope
- ❌ Heartbeat / EKG line
- ❌ DNA double helix
- ❌ Caduceus / Rod of Asclepius / serpent-and-staff
- ❌ Pill, capsule, or tablet
- ❌ Tooth silhouette (for the dental angle)
- ❌ Chat bubble / speech bubble
- ❌ Robot head, AI brain, neural network nodes
- ❌ Hexagon tech-grid pattern
- ❌ Abstract "swoosh" or "swoosh-people" (the smiling-person-arc logo)
- ❌ Globe with a cursor arrow on it

### BANNED COLOR MOVES
- ❌ Blue + white + teal. This is the healthcare default and it is invisible.
- ❌ Soft gradient from cyan to purple (the "tech startup" tell)
- ❌ Glossy 3D gradient orbs or blobs
- ❌ Mint green "wellness" palette

### BANNED PHOTOGRAPHY
- ❌ Stock doctor with arms crossed, white coat, forced smile
- ❌ Handshake in a hospital corridor
- ❌ Stethoscope draped on a clipboard
- ❌ Diverse team of doctors laughing in slow motion

### BANNED TYPOGRAPHY
- ❌ Rounded geometric sans with a dot on the "i" as a logo mark
- ❌ Anything that looks like it was assembled from a Tailwind template
- ❌ Inter / Poppins / Montserrat used as the identity typeface

## 5. THE CREATIVE TERRITORY

### TERRITORY A — "THE FILLED SLOT"  ⭐ RECOMMENDED
**The big idea:** FJMK's product is a booked appointment. Their client's problem is the empty
slot in the schedule. So the identity is built on **the grid, the slot, and the filled vs.
the empty.**

**Why it works:**
- It is a literal diagram of the business proposition, not a decoration.
- The name is four letters — **F J M K** — which maps perfectly to a **2×2 grid of cells.**
  The logo is the name arranged as the schedule itself, with one cell filled.
- The mark reduces to a 2×2 grid with one cell lit — readable at 16px as a favicon.
- Nothing about it is medical. It is *operational*. That is the non-generic move: FJMK is
  not selling healthcare, it is selling a full calendar.

**Visual register:** private-clinic luxury meets editorial design studio. Dark, quiet,
expensive. The palette is the anti-healthcare palette.

**Motifs:** the 2×2 grid, tick marks, timestamps, booking-confirmation language,
"available / booked" states, the vertical rule, generous empty space (empty space = the
empty slots, and the brand's job is to fill them).

---

### TERRITORY B — "CLINICAL PRECISION"
**The big idea:** hospital-grade trust through Swiss discipline. Strictly two colors plus one
surgical accent. Extreme type-scale contrast (a 60px headline next to a 9px label).

**Visual register:** Swiss institutional, systematic, cold-clean, rigorous.
**Motifs:** crosshairs, measurement rules, specimen labels, serial numbers, index cards.
**Risk:** can tip into feeling cold and corporate — which is exactly the stereotype FJMK
should be escaping. Use only if Territory A feels too clever.

---

### TERRITORY C — "THE WAITING ROOM"
**The big idea:** the opposite of sterile. Warm off-white paper, deep ink, one warm accent
(terracotta or amber). Soft light, human, calm, reassuring.

**Visual register:** humanist editorial, warm, tactile, printed-paper feel.
**Motifs:** soft shadow, paper grain, quiet photography of real spaces, warm neutrals.
**Risk:** warm + soft is a crowded wellness space; harder to make distinctive. The grid
concept in Territory A has more ownable intellectual property.

## 6. RECOMMENDED VISUAL SYSTEM (Territory A)

### COLOR
| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Ink | `#0A0A0A` | Primary background |
| Surface | Char | `#141414` | Cards, panels |
| Surface alt | Slate | `#1A1A1A` | Image wells |
| Primary text | Bone | `#F5F5F5` | Headlines, body |
| Secondary text | Ash | `#888888` | Captions, labels |
| Accent | Brass | `#D4A853` | The "filled slot." Used sparingly — 5% of any surface. |
| Accent hover | Brass Light | `#E5B96A` | Interactive states |
| Rule | Hairline | `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` | Borders, dividers |

**Discipline rule:** brass is the filled slot. It appears once per composition. If everything
is gold, nothing is gold.

**Optional secondary accent** for charts/data only: a clinical signal green `#39EB72` or
alarm orange `#FF4B14`. Never in the logo.

### TYPOGRAPHY
| Role | Face | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Fraunces** (or GT Sectra / Editorial New feel) | High-contrast serif. Warm, editorial, expensive. Says "design studio," not "hospital." |
| Body | **Inter** | Neutral workhorse. Deliberately plain so the display carries the personality. |
| Labels / data | **JetBrains Mono** | Timestamps, step numbers, tags. Reinforces the scheduling/operational idea. |

**Type rules:** display set light (300) at large sizes with tight tracking. Mono always
uppercase, wide letter-spacing (2–3px), small (10–12px). Body light (300) — never bold.

### LOGO CONCEPT
**Primary lockup:** the wordmark **FJMK** in the display serif, light weight, with a
hairline 2×2 grid structure implied behind or beside it, one cell filled brass.

**The mark (icon only):** a 2×2 grid of four cells, three outlined in bone, **one filled
brass.** No letters. This is the favicon, the app icon, the social avatar, the stamp.

**Alternates to explore:**
1. Four cells, letters sit *inside* the cells — grid as a letterform cage
2. Letters as a horizontal wordmark, grid mark to the left as a separate symbol
3. A single rounded-rect "slot" split by a vertical rule, one half brass (simpler, softer)
4. Stacked vertical lockup for narrow formats

**Technical rules for the logo:**
- Flat vector. No gradients, no bevels, no drop shadows, no 3D.
- Must work at 16px, in one color, reversed on dark, and on light.
- Must work without the brass (pure mono version) for faxes, stamps, embroidery.
- **Do not ask an image AI to render the letterforms.** Diffusion models mangle text.
  AI generates the *mood and direction*; the actual logo is drawn as vector (SVG) by hand.

### TEXTURE & MOTION
- Subtle film grain at 3–5% opacity over dark grounds — kills the "flat vector" cheapness.
- Motion: reveals that slide up from behind a mask; a counter that ticks; a grid cell that
  fills. Motion should feel like a *system filling up*, not like decoration.
- No bouncing, no springy easing. Easing is `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — fast out, slow in.

## 8. HOW TO FEED THIS TO GPT

Paste the whole brief above, then use a prompt like this:

> Using the FJMK brand brief above, act as an art director. Produce **5 distinct creative
> directions** for the brand. For each direction give me:
> 1. A name and a one-sentence concept
> 2. The big idea in one line
> 3. Palette with hex codes
> 4. Typeface pairing with reasoning
> 5. The logo concept described in structural terms (shapes, grid, negative space) — not a
>    vague mood
> 6. **An image-generation prompt** (Midjourney / Flux / DALL·E style) for a poster that
>    expresses the direction — include subject, composition, style, medium, lighting,
>    color, and a separate **negative prompt** list
> 7. A **negative prompt** block that explicitly excludes every item in the BANNED list
>    from the brief
>
> Then rank the 5 directions by how non-generic they are and tell me which one to build.

**Then, for the logo specifically, run a second pass:**

> Now take the winning direction and write me: (a) a precise vector specification for the
> logo — exact geometry, proportions, grid construction, corner radii, stroke weights —
> something a designer could build in Illustrator or I could build in SVG; and (b) three
> variations of the mark that keep the core idea but change the construction.

**Then, for posters and social:**

> Write 6 image-generation prompts for: (1) the manifesto poster, (2) a clinic waiting-room
> print piece, (3) an Instagram carousel cover, (4) a LinkedIn banner, (5) a pitch-deck
> cover, (6) a case-study hero image. Each prompt must specify aspect ratio, composition
> rule, lighting, palette (hex), texture, and a negative prompt.

**Critical instruction to include in every image prompt request:**

> The image must not contain any medical iconography, no crosses, no stethoscopes, no
> heartbeat lines, no doctors, no blue-and-white healthcare palette, no gradients, no
> glossy 3D shapes, and no rendered text — leave text out entirely, I will set type myself.

## 9. THE ONE-LINE TEST

Before approving any asset, ask:

> **Does this look like every other healthcare agency, or does it look like a design studio
> that happens to have clinic clients?**

If it looks like every other healthcare agency, it is wrong.
