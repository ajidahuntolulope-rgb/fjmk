How Much Does a Professional Website Cost in Nigeria in 2026?

OA
Oluwatobi Ajidahun
FJMK · Buying guide

A professional website in Nigeria in 2026 costs between ₦150,000 and ₦2,500,000 to build, depending on what you actually need. The range is that wide because "a website" describes everything from a five-page brochure site to a custom platform with online booking, patient records, and payment integration. They are not remotely the same job.

This guide gives you the real 2026 price ranges, what drives the price, what the recurring costs are, and the questions to ask before you pay anyone — so you can compare quotes on an identical scope instead of comparing apples to oranges.

The 2026 price ranges

These are current market ranges for Nigerian agencies and developers, compiled from multiple 2026 pricing guides and cross-checked. [musskart.com; linestech.com.ng; archypage.com]

| Website type | Typical build cost (₦) | Typical timeline |

|---|---|---|

| Landing page / one-pager | ₦80,000 – ₦200,000 | 3–7 days |

| Basic business website (5–12 pages, template or WordPress) | ₦150,000 – ₦650,000 | 1–3 weeks |

| Professional custom-designed website | ₦500,000 – ₦2,500,000 | 3–8 weeks |

| Hospital or clinic website | ₦350,000 – ₦900,000 | 2–6 weeks |

| E-commerce website | ₦400,000 – ₦3,500,000+ | 4–12 weeks |

| Custom web application (portals, booking systems, dashboards) | ₦1,500,000+ | 8 weeks+ |

Small clinics can launch well from around ₦200,000; a multi-department hospital with appointment systems and a patient portal starts from roughly ₦450,000 and rises with the features. [dpx.com.ng; archypage.com]

What actually drives the price

Five things move the number. Understand these and a quote stops being a mystery:

1. Page count and content. A 5-page site and a 20-page site are different jobs. Every service page, doctor profile, and location page is design, writing, and structure. The cheapest way to control cost is to know exactly which pages you need before you ask for a quote.

2. Custom vs template. A template-based build (WordPress theme) is faster and cheaper. A custom design — built to your brand, not a theme everyone else uses — costs more. For a clinic, the question isn't "which is prettier" but "which one will patients trust." A generic template that five other clinics in your city are using undermines trust; a clean custom site builds it.

3. Features. This is where healthcare sites diverge from brochure sites. Online appointment booking, a patient portal, secure forms, payment integration (Paystack/Flutterwave), WhatsApp integration, HMO/insurance pages — each is a feature with a cost. Booking systems and patient portals are the big add-ons for clinics and hospitals.

4. Content and copywriting. A website is not a design deliverable; it is a set of answers to patient questions. Professional copywriting — service pages that explain what happens at a visit, doctor profiles, FAQ content — is real work and real cost. Sites that skip it look good and convert nothing.

5. Who builds it. A freelancer, a small agency, and a big agency have different rates, different processes, and different accountability. The cheap build is not necessarily the bad one — but a ₦50,000 website and a ₦500,000 website are different products, and pretending otherwise is how businesses get burned.

The recurring costs nobody tells you about

A website is not a one-off purchase. It is an asset that must be kept online, secure, and current. Budget for these on top of the build:

| Item | Indicative 2026 cost |

|---|---|

| Domain name | ₦3,000 – ₦30,000 per year (.com.ng is cheapest; .com is priced in US dollars) |

| Hosting (shared) | ₦20,000 – ₦120,000 per year |

| Hosting (VPS/cloud, for high-traffic or custom systems) | ₦150,000 – ₦800,000+ per year |

| SSL certificate | Often free with hosting — verify it's included |

| Maintenance / care plan | ₦20,000 – ₦150,000 per month (updates, backups, security, small changes) |

| Business email | Free to a few thousand naira per user per month |

[linestech.com.ng; archypage.com]

A typical small business should plan for roughly ₦50,000 – ₦150,000 a year in running costs on top of the build, more if you take a monthly care plan. Payment gateway fees (Paystack, Flutterwave) are separate again — a percentage per transaction, not a website cost.

The exchange-rate trap

Domains ending in .com, premium themes, most plugins, Google Workspace, international hosting, and many SaaS tools are priced in US dollars. Their naira cost moves with the exchange rate at every renewal — a ₦30,000 hosting bill in 2024 can become ₦60,000 in 2026 without the vendor doing anything.

Before you sign anything, ask which recurring items are dollar-denominated. Prefer .com.ng domains where appropriate, consider Nigerian hosting billed in naira, and build the exchange-rate risk into your budget. A vendor who can't tell you which of your costs are dollar-priced doesn't understand your real bill.

What a "cheap website" actually costs you

A ₦50,000–₦100,000 website is not a bargain; it is usually a brochure with a contact form, built from a template, with no strategy behind it. The real cost shows up later:

  • It doesn't answer patient questions, so it doesn't convert — every visitor it fails to turn into a booking is lost revenue, forever.
  • It's slow on mobile data, so patients leave before it loads.
  • It has no analytics, so you can't see what's working.
  • It has no maintenance, so it gets hacked or breaks and you find out when a patient complains.
  • It looks like every other template site, so it actively damages the trust you're trying to build.

The average healthcare website converts about 3% of visitors into enquiries; a focused, well-built one runs closer to 7%. [First Page Sage 2025] The difference between those two numbers is not design taste — it is booked appointments. A website that converts at 7% instead of 3% pays for its own build cost many times over, which is why the question "how much does a website cost" is the wrong question. The right question is "what is this website going to do for my revenue, and what is that worth?"

Questions to ask before you pay anyone

Get written quotes on an identical scope, and ask:

  1. What exactly is included? Page count, pages list, features, content — in writing.
  2. Who writes the content? If you must supply it, budget for it. If they write it, is it included?
  3. What are the recurring costs? Domain, hosting, SSL, maintenance — and which are dollar-denominated?
  4. What happens after launch? Training, handover, support period, who fixes a bug in month three?
  5. Is it built to convert or just to look good? Ask what the site is designed to do for patient bookings, not just how many pages it has.
  6. Who owns what? Domain, hosting account, and code should be in your name or transferred to you — a site you don't own is a liability.
  7. Can I see real examples? Not mockups — live sites they built, for businesses like yours, that you can visit on your phone.

How much does a hospital website cost in Nigeria?

Indicatively ₦350,000 – ₦900,000 for a clinic or hospital site, with smaller clinics from around ₦200,000. Appointment booking systems and patient portals add to that. [archypage.com; dpx.com.ng]

Why are website prices so different?

Page count, custom vs template design, features (booking, payments, portals), content quality, and who builds it. Compare quotes on identical scope, not identical price.

Do I need to pay for hosting and domain separately?

Yes — those are recurring costs on top of the build: roughly ₦3,000–₦30,000/year for the domain and ₦20,000–₦120,000/year for hosting.

Can I build a website myself to save money?

You can, with Wix/Squarespace/WordPress — and many businesses should start there. What you give up is conversion engineering, local SEO setup, booking integration, and maintenance. If your website is a serious revenue channel, the build pays for itself.

What's the cheapest website that actually works?

A clean one-page site with a booking button, real photos, reviews, and HMO info — built to load fast on mobile data — will outperform a 20-page template site with no strategy. Scope matters more than size.

How much should I budget per year to keep a website running?

Roughly ₦50,000 – ₦150,000 a year on top of the build for domain, hosting, and basic maintenance — more if you take a care plan or run a custom system.

What to do next

Before you pay anyone, write down the exact pages and features your business needs — that single document will save you more than any discount. Then get two or three written quotes on that identical scope and compare them line by line.
