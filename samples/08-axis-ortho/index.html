How to read this

Specified, not described.

Most orthopaedic websites tell you that a knee replacement "restores mobility". That is not information. This page tells you the implant material, the approach, the anaesthetic, the length of stay, and the expected return to work — because those are the things a patient actually needs to decide.

We publish our revision rate. Not every centre will. We think a surgeon who will not tell you how often their operations need redoing has not earned your trust yet.

Data note

All figures on this page are illustrative and typical for a centre of this kind. Individual outcomes depend on the patient, the indication and the implant used.

Fig. 02 — Procedure specifications

Datasheets.

Each procedure specified the way a component would be: approach, materials, anaesthesia, stay, and return to function.

PRC-01
Total knee

Total knee replacement

Resurfacing the three compartments of the knee with metal and polyethylene components. For end-stage osteoarthritis where conservative management has failed.

ApproachMedial parapatellar
ImplantCobalt-chrome femoral, titanium tibial, XLPE insert
FixationCemented
AnaesthesiaSpinal + adductor canal block
Hospital stay2–3 nights
Weight bearingFull, day of surgery
Return to driving6 weeks
Return to work (sedentary)6–8 weeks
PRC-02
Total hip

Total hip replacement

Replacing the femoral head and acetabular socket. One of the most reliable operations in medicine, with a long documented survival profile.

ApproachPosterior or direct lateral
ImplantTitanium stem, ceramic or metal head, XLPE liner
FixationCemented or uncemented
AnaesthesiaSpinal
Hospital stay2–3 nights
Weight bearingFull, day of surgery
Return to driving6 weeks
Dislocation risk<2% with modern bearings
PRC-03
Decompression

Spinal decompression

Relieving pressure on nerve roots or the spinal cord caused by disc herniation or stenosis. Performed where conservative treatment has not resolved radicular symptoms.

ApproachPosterior, minimally invasive where suitable
LevelsUsually 1–2
AnaesthesiaGeneral
Hospital stay1–2 nights
MobilisationDay of surgery
Return to desk work3–4 weeks
Return to manual work8–12 weeks
Success rate80–90% for leg pain
PRC-04
Arthroscopy

Knee & shoulder arthroscopy

Keyhole surgery for meniscal tears, ligament reconstruction and shoulder impingement. Diagnostic and therapeutic in the same procedure.

Portals2–3, each ~5mm
AnaesthesiaSpinal or general
Hospital stayDay case
Weight bearingDepends on procedure
Return to sport6 weeks (meniscectomy) to 9 months (ACL)
Wound careWaterproof dressings, shower next day
PRC-05
Fixation

Fracture fixation

Internal fixation of long bone and periarticular fractures using plates, screws, nails or external fixation depending on the fracture pattern and soft tissue envelope.

ImplantTitanium or stainless steel
Fixation typePlate / intramedullary nail / ex-fix
AnaesthesiaSpinal or general
Hospital stay1–5 nights depending on injury
Union time6–12 weeks
Implant removalOnly if symptomatic

Fig. 04 — Treatment pathway

From referral
to discharge.

01

Consultation

History, examination, and imaging review. We tell you what is wrong and what the options are.

02

Workup

Bloods, ECG, anaesthetic review, and templating of the implant against your own imaging.

03

Pre-hab

Strength and education before surgery. Patients who pre-hab recover measurably faster.

04

Procedure

Performed to the specification in Fig. 02. Implant details recorded to your file and the registry.

05

Rehabilitation

Physiotherapy from day one, then structured follow-up at 2, 6 and 12 weeks, then annually.
