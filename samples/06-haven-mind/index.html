What we help with

You do not need a
diagnosis to come in.

People arrive here for all sorts of reasons, and most of them are not dramatic. If something is sitting with you and you cannot put it down, that is reason enough.

Anxiety and worry

The kind that keeps you awake at 2am rehearsing conversations, or makes your chest tight before something ordinary.

Low mood and depression

When things you used to enjoy have gone flat, or getting through a day feels like more effort than it should.

Trauma and difficult memories

Things that happened, that you have coped with, that still arrive uninvited. You do not have to describe them on the first day.

Relationships and family

Couples work, family strain, the particular weight of expectations in a Nigerian household.

Adolescents and young people

For teenagers who will not talk to their parents, and for parents who do not know how to reach them.

Grief and loss

Bereavement, miscarriage, the end of something that mattered. Grief does not follow a schedule.

Work and burnout

The exhaustion that sleep does not fix, and the guilt about feeling it when you are, by every outward measure, doing well.

Life transitions

New country, new baby, new job, retirement, divorce. Change is not always a crisis, but it is always an adjustment.

Not sure yet

If none of these fit and you cannot explain why you are here, come anyway. That is a very common way to start.

How it works

What actually happens,
step by step.

The unknown is usually worse than the thing itself. So here is exactly what the process looks like.

01

A short call first

Fifteen minutes, free, no obligation. You tell us roughly what is going on and we tell you honestly whether we are the right people and who would be the best match. If we are not, we will say so and suggest somewhere that is.

02

Your first session

Fifty minutes. Nobody makes you talk about anything you are not ready to. A lot of first sessions are spent on what brought you here and what you would like to be different. You can stop at any point.

03

Finding the shape of it

The next few sessions are about understanding the pattern — not just what is happening, but how it works and what keeps it going. This is usually where people start to feel less alone in it.

04

Doing the work

Weekly at first, then less often as things settle. Some people come for six sessions, some for a year. There is no minimum and no contract, and you can pause whenever you need to.

05

Ending well

Therapy should end. When you are ready, we plan the last few sessions properly rather than letting it trail off — because how something ends matters as much as how it started.

Confidentiality and ethics

What you say here
stays here.

This is the part people worry about most, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassuring one.

What you tell your therapist is confidential. We do not tell your family, your employer, your church, your doctor or anyone else. We do not keep records that anyone outside the practice can read.

There are three narrow legal exceptions, and you deserve to know them before you begin rather than after.

  • If there is an immediate risk to your life or someone else's, we may need to involve emergency services — and we would tell you we were doing it.
  • If a child is at risk of serious harm, we have a legal duty to report it.
  • If a court orders disclosure of records, we must comply.

Outside those three things, nothing leaves the room. If you have questions about this, ask them on the first call — before you have told us anything.

All our clinicians are registered and supervised, and work to a professional code of ethics. Supervision itself is confidential and anonymised.

If you are in crisis right now

Please do not wait for an appointment. This section is here so that anyone who needs it can find it quickly.

  • If you are in immediate danger of harming yourself or someone else, go to the nearest emergency department or call the emergency services on 112.
  • If you are having thoughts of suicide, tell someone you trust and stay with them until you have spoken to a professional.
  • The Lagos State mental health helpline operates 24 hours. Ask for the crisis line when you call.
  • If you are safe but struggling, request an appointment below and mark it urgent — we hold slots each week for exactly this.
