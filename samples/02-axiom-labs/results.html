How to read a flag

Normal means the result sits inside the reference range for that analyte. Borderline means it is close to the edge and worth watching over time rather than acting on immediately. Above or below range means it falls outside — which is a prompt for a conversation with your clinician, not a diagnosis in itself.

What a reference range actually is

A reference range is built by measuring a large population of apparently healthy people and taking the central 95%. That means, by construction, one in twenty healthy people falls outside it on any given test. A single out-of-range value is common and often means nothing. A pattern across several results over time is what matters.

Why we publish the range

Because a result without its range is not information. "Cholesterol 6.1" is a number. "Cholesterol 6.1 against a target of under 5.2" is a fact you can act on. Most lab reports make you look the range up yourself. We put it on the line.

Sharing with your doctor

Every report is released to your nominated clinician at the same moment it is released to you, by email, with the full data set. If you would rather your doctor saw it first, that is a setting on your account.
